# Scenario Guides

Collect the facts required for the scenario before drafting. When editing, prioritize the scenario’s highest-risk signals. The suggested structures are information skeletons that help readers complete a task, not mandatory templates.

## Feature Launches and Releases

**Source material:** actual changes, affected users, version and release date, migration or upgrade actions, compatibility, known limitations, and availability channels.

**Structure:** begin with the change users will notice; explain how to use or upgrade; then state limits, migration, and follow-up action.

**Prioritize:** internal implementation presented as user value, exaggerated benefits, omitted compatibility or limits, and planned functionality presented as released.

## User Documentation and Guides

**Source material:** reader task, prerequisites, UI or commands, expected result, permissions, failure paths, and related links.

**Structure:** goal and scope → prerequisites → shortest working steps → result verification → limitations or troubleshooting.

**Prioritize:** feature descriptions standing in for instructions, assumed context, and missing dangerous-operation or recovery guidance.

## Technical Documentation and READMEs

**Source material:** audience, runtime environment, installation and configuration, interface or architecture boundaries, executable examples, and maintenance or compatibility constraints.

**Structure:** problem and applicability → shortest working path → rationale, architecture, and advanced configuration.

**Prioritize:** abstract architecture narration, generic “best practices,” stale examples, and implementation choices presented as universal truth.

## Product Pages, Marketing Pages, Emails, and Announcements

**Source material:** defined audience, desired action, real pain point, product mechanism, differentiation, proof, limitations, and CTA.

**Structure:** concrete reader outcome → scenario and mechanism → evidence or objection handling → unambiguous next step.

**Prioritize:** value claims applicable to every product, adjective stacks, unsupported comparisons or numbers, and vague CTAs.

## Product and Technical Blogs, Retrospectives, and Opinion Pieces

**Source material:** central claim, target reader, real material, counterexamples or limits, author judgment, and relevant links or evidence.

**Structure:** open with a concrete problem, observation, or conclusion; develop it through process, evidence, and examples; close with an action, judgment, or open question.

**Prioritize:** generic openings, encyclopedic background, benefit-only lists, unsupported claims, and conclusions that repeat the entire article.

## In-Product Microcopy

**Source material:** current screen and user state, available action, action result, error cause, risk and recovery path, and character constraints.

**Structure:** state or action first; explain the reason when needed; make the next step clear. Errors should help recovery; warnings should name irreversible consequences.

**Prioritize:** polite but vague wording, internal errors exposed to users, button labels that do not predict their result, and empty states without a next action.

## Internal Communication, RFCs, Project Updates, and Retrospectives

**Source material:** problem, decision, alternatives, evidence, risk, owner, status, and next step.

**Structure:** lead with the decision or current status; explain rationale and trade-offs; list risks, unresolved items, and named owners.

**Prioritize:** corporate language that hides the conclusion, missing decision rationale, unclear ownership, and uncertainty disguised as certainty.

## Localization and Bilingual Content

**Source material:** source intent, target audience, terminology, UI length constraints, compliance or risk disclosure, and published comparable translations.

**Structure:** reorganize for the target language and channel while keeping behavior, scope, limits, terminology, and required action consistent.

**Prioritize:** literal syntax, drifting translations, channel-inappropriate tone, and non-equivalent risk disclosure across Chinese and English.
