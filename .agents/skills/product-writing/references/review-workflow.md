# Editing and Review Workflow

## Diagnose

Identify the content type and reader task, then select the one to three signals from [AI Writing Signals](ai-writing-signals.md) that most affect the reader. Do not inventory every suspicious word. Point to the specific issue that can mislead, slow comprehension, weaken trust, or drift from the intended voice.

For each issue, use:

```text
Issue: <specific passage or problem type>
Why it matters: <effect on the reader, facts, or voice>
Suggested change: <delete, add evidence, restructure, or rephrase>
```

## Light Edit

Use when facts and structure are largely correct and the text needs clearer language or a more consistent voice.

- Preserve the author’s position, terminology, information order, and necessary detail.
- Remove filler, repetition, and unsupported intensifiers.
- Replace abstraction with concrete actions or outcomes supported by source material.
- Do not remove prerequisites, limits, risks, or recovery paths merely to make prose shorter.

## Deep Edit

Use when the content has no reader path, material factual gaps, template-driven structure, or an obviously inconsistent voice.

- Propose the new information structure and explain why it is needed.
- Reorder facts, reader questions, limits, and actions.
- Mark unverified sections as material still needed rather than filling them with fluent generalities.
- Preserve valuable professional judgment and verifiable detail from the original.

## Final Review

Confirm that:

1. claims, capabilities, data, dates, versions, and links are supported by source material;
2. the reader can complete the goal, or understands why they cannot yet do so;
3. important prerequisites, permissions, limits, risks, and recovery paths were not obscured during editing;
4. headings, paragraphs, and lists follow the reader’s information dependencies rather than a completeness template;
5. the text has no clusters of empty language, formulaic transitions, repeated sentence shapes, or unnecessary rhetoric;
6. vocabulary and tone match the channel and established voice; and
7. bilingual versions are equivalent in facts and risk disclosure.
