# Source Material, Terminology, and Voice

## Evidence Priority

For product behavior and release status, prioritize merged implementations and tests, approved specifications, finalized designs, official product data, and user-confirmed information. Historical documentation, commit subjects, and informal summaries are discovery leads; they do not independently support high-risk or external claims.

Classify material as:

- **Confirmed**: safe to state in publishable copy.
- **To confirm**: present as a question, explicit placeholder, or conditional statement.
- **Do not infer**: omit from external copy.

Never add figures, customer endorsements, performance improvements, release timing, compatibility, or future commitments merely to make a piece feel complete.

## Voice Assets

Extract reusable decisions—not wording—from a small sample of strong reference material:

- audience and degree of directness;
- preferred sentence length, heading style, and information density;
- established and prohibited terminology;
- use of first person, humor, technical detail, or explicit judgment; and
- how tone changes across documentation, blogs, marketing, and in-product copy.

When no samples exist, select a plain channel-appropriate voice: documentation prioritizes clarity and accuracy; releases prioritize change and impact; marketing prioritizes specific user value and proof; internal writing prioritizes directness and accountability. Do not fabricate experience or emotion to manufacture a voice.

## Terminology and Localization

Reuse established product names, feature names, menu labels, commands, API names, and version formats. When terminology conflicts, flag the conflict and seek an authoritative source; do not create competing terms on your own.

Chinese and English content must be equivalent in feature scope, prerequisites, risks, and required action. Sentence order, headings, and examples may differ by language; sentence-by-sentence alignment is not required.
